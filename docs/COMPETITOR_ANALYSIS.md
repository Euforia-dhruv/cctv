# RetailVision AI: Competitor and Market Analysis

Qualitative analysis based on public product knowledge (2026). No confidential data used. Prices and features change; re-verify before procurement decisions.

## 1. Landscape

The retail video-analytics market splits into two eras: hardware-centric first-generation players and newer software/cloud platforms. Our positioning is a software-first platform that works with existing CCTV and a deployed demo footprint (single laptop), which is the fastest credible entry for a pilot.

| Player | Offering | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RetailNext | Store traffic analytics (Wi-Fi + video) | SaaS + on-prem sensor hardware; footfall, conversion, pathing | Strong for large chains; hardware-heavy, enterprise pricing; [knowledge] |
| ShopperTrak / Sensormatic | Counters + analytics | Hardware sensors and cloud dashboard | counts primarily; less video-native [knowledge] |
| Dor Technologies (Dor) | AI visitor analytics for retail | Cloud SaaS, camera-agnostic | strong analytics, US-market pricing [knowledge] |
| FootfallCam | 3D people counting + heatmaps | Edge camera hardware | recognized in APAC/MEA; requires their hardware [knowledge] |
| Verint | Loss prevention + operational video | Enterprise VMS-adjacent | strong in investigation, expensive [knowledge] |
| GridSmart / traffic-data vendors | Public-space analytics | edge | less retail-specific [knowledge] |
| Indian players (e.g., Staqu, analytics vendors) | AI video analytics | enterprise | mostly surveillance-first [knowledge] |

## 2. Gap we are addressing

- Most affordable video analytics for retailers is either tied to proprietary hardware or priced for chains with hundreds of stores.
- Small and mid-size Indian retail (DMart-style supermarkets, malls, standalone stores) has existing CCTV, limited IT, and no analytics layer.
- RetailVision AI's wedge: upload an existing recording or connect one camera, get real CV-based insight (traffic, dwell, heatmap, congestion, operational alerts, review workflow) in a CRM-style dashboard, at a fraction of the cost, with an honest evidence model.

## 3. Differentiation

1. Works with existing footage (upload) and existing cameras; no hardware to buy.
2. Honest analytics: detections vs unique visits clearly separated, exposure estimates labeled as estimates, no fabricated queue/count claims.
3. Human-in-the-loop incident review with neutral labels, never auto-accusation.
4. Privacy by design: no facial recognition, no biometrics, anonymous track IDs only.
5. Lightweight: runs on a single laptop for demos; architecture lifts to cloud GPU.

## 4. Risks

- Cloud incumbents (RetailNext, Dor) have mature dashboards; we must win on price, speed to value, and India-specific support.
- Retailers are sensitive about surveillance optics; the review workflow and privacy position are a feature, not a footnote.
- Accuracy expectations: we must set expectations (counting under occlusion, single camera) clearly; that is why estimates carry confidence and limitations.

## 5. Pricing model for future (not implemented)

- Pilot: per-store per-month SaaS + one-time setup; demo tier free.
- Value metric: stores, cameras, video minutes analyzed, or flat per-store. Decide after pilot feedback.
